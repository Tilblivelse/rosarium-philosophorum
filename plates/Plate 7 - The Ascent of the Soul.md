# Plate 7: The Ascent of the Soul

## Image
![Plate 7](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS07.jpg)

Image link: [RS07.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS07.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

A lifeless corpse lies below, with a small human figure (ARAS calls it a homunculus) rising from it and moving upward toward heaven. ARAS describes the four elements as shown divided or separated. The soul leaves from what ARAS calls the two that have become one. No further inscriptions, colours or placement are given by ARAS.

Source of this description: [ARAS concordance](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-7-ascent-soul). Inscriptions are as reported by ARAS in English and have not been checked against the print.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the ARAS concordance of Jung's Psychology of the Transference ([ARAS concordance, Rosarium 7](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-7-ascent-soul)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210, illustration 7: titled "The Extraction or Impregnation of the Soule"; it adds a tree growing from the central figure's abdomen, which Glasgow says is not in the original text ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)).
