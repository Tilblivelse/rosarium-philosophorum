# Plate 18: The Green Lion Devours the Sun

## Image
A green lion sits on the ground, head raised, eating the sun.

## Text and motto
No verse or motto cited in the sources consulted.

## Alchemical stage
Not named

### Etymology and symbolism
- Dissolve: Latin dissolvere, 'to loosen up, break apart', from dis- and solvere ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/dissolve)). Glasgow reads the lion devouring the sun as gold being dissolved or purified in a powerful solvent, releasing the seed from which gold may be grown.
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Reading (Henderson, second series)
Joseph L. Henderson, presentation of 18 March 1990 on the second half of the series, summarised by Daniel S. Benveniste (ARAS Connections 2023, Issue 2).

- Henderson: the green lion is nature and the sun is spirit: 'nature is eating the spirit, and that is what might happen if the unconscious were activated too completely'. He urges the alchemists' attitude of 'patience in enduring the whole process as it unfolds'.

## Jung
Jung's Psychology of the Transference (1946) comments on plates 1-10 only.

## Other witnesses and notes
- Glasgow: a classic alchemical symbol; the gold or sun is dissolved or purified in a powerful solvent so that the seed from which pure gold may be grown is released; the images have many possible meanings.
- Adam McLean, commentary in the Magnum Opus edition of the Rosary of the Philosophers (Edinburgh, 1980), hosted on alchemywebsite.com. Layer 3, one author's view. McLean reads all twenty woodcuts as one pattern (Body, Soul and Spirit) and presents his reading as one interpretation among others. What he says about this plate specifically has not yet been read.
