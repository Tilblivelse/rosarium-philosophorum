# Plate 3: Naked Truth

## Image
Sol and Luna stand naked, facing each other. Their hands are arranged around three branches bearing the flores Mercurii, corresponding to the three pipes of the fountain. A dove above carries the inscription "Spiritus est qui unificat". The figure has six rays, not eight.

## Text and motto
Text adapted from the Tractatus aureus: the initiate is humble, devout, cheerful and respectful, and fears God. The Rosarium adds a line from Pseudo-Aristotle: "Could God but find a man of faithful understanding, he would open his secret to him." Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 records scrolls: Sol, "O Luna, let me be thy husband"; Luna, "O Sol, I must submit to thee"; the bird, "It is the Spirit which vivifies."

## Alchemical stage
Preparation for the coniunctio (ARAS names no operation)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Spirit: Latin spiritus, 'breath', from spirare, 'to breathe' ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/spirit)). On this plate the dove carries the inscription 'Spiritus est qui unificat' (ARAS), and Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 gives the bird's scroll as 'It is the Spirit which vivifies'.
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶451-452)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) The opus is moral and psychological; the dove as mediator points to union in the spirit. (a-1) Left-hand contact has ended; each now holds the branches with the opposite hand. (a-2) Three flowers replace five; the eightfold figure becomes sixfold, prepared by Maria's axiom. (a-3) Joannes Lydus on six as both even and odd, linked to Aphrodite and Phosphorus/Hesperus.
- (b) 'Man stands as he is': the shadow revealed and integrated into the ego; wholeness as completeness, not perfection. (b-1) Humility before the shadow; projection and the hidden 'incest' attraction remain dangers.
- (c) Ego and shadow united in a fragile unity; the unconscious intensifies attraction; the alchemical fire must start gently and rise.

McLean reads all twenty woodcuts as one pattern (Body, Soul and Spirit) and presents his reading as one interpretation among others. What he says about this plate specifically has not yet been read. A general argument that Jung chose the Rosarium drawings as a template for the analytic process, shaped by his relationships with Spielrein, Moltzer and Wolff; it is not a reading of this plate.
