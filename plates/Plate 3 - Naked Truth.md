# Plate 3: Naked Truth

## Image
![Plate 3](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS03.jpg)

Image link: [RS03.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS03.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

The king and queen (Sol and Luna) face each other openly. Luna's left hand and Sol's right hand hold the branches; Luna's right hand and Sol's left hand touch the flowers, so the left-to-left clasp of the previous picture is gone. The branches bear three flowers, not five, and the figure is described as six-rayed. A dove is inscribed "Spiritus est qui unificat" (as ARAS gives it). A further inscription marks the remaining pair of opposites, masculine and feminine or agens and patiens. ARAS quotes speech from Sol and Luna but does not say whether those words appear in the picture.

Source of this description: [ARAS concordance](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-3-naked-truth). Inscriptions are as reported by ARAS in English and have not been checked against the print.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the ARAS concordance of Jung's Psychology of the Transference ([ARAS concordance, Rosarium 3](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-3-naked-truth)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210, illustration 3: captioned "The King and Queen disrobe and begin to come together" ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)). The scrolls attributed to Sol and Luna are reported by Glasgow in a passage that covers illustrations 2 and 3 together, so their placement is unconfirmed. The bird's scroll is given there as "It is the Spirit which vivifies".
