# Plate 6: Death

## Image
In the vas hermeticum picture, the fountain and sea have become a sarcophagus and tomb. The king and queen lie dead, merged into a single two-headed being (the dead hermaphrodite).

## Text and motto
Verse (English rendering quoted by ARAS): "Here King and Queen are lying dead / In great distress the soul is sped".

## Alchemical stage
Putrefactio / nigredo; picture also titled 'Conceptio' (ARAS)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Putrefaction: Latin putrefacere, 'to make rotten' ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/putrefaction)). ARAS names the stage of this plate putrefactio / nigredo, and shows the king and queen dead and merged into one two-headed being.
- Mortification: Late Latin mortificatio, 'a killing, putting to death', from mortificare ('make dead') and mors, 'death'; in late 14th-century English its religious sense was the subduing of bodily appetites ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/mortification)). Etymonline does not give an alchemical sense, so none is claimed here.
- Sarcophagus: Greek sarkophagos (lithos), 'limestone used for coffins', literally 'flesh-eating' from sarx ('flesh') and phagein ('to eat'), from the belief that the stone rapidly decayed corpses ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/sarcophagus)). On this plate the fountain and sea of Plate 1 have become a sarcophagus and tomb (ARAS).
- Nigredo: no etymology was sourced; Wikipedia glosses the term as 'blackness' and says it means putrefaction or decomposition in alchemy, and, in analytical psychology, a 'dark night of the soul' in which a person faces the shadow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigredo)).
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶466-474)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) Deathlike stillness after the coniunctio; an interim stage before new life, as in the sower's grain.
- (b) The corpse as a new bisexual body, the rebis or lapis, not yet alive. (b-1) Death as tacit punishment for incest ('the wages of sin is death'); blackness requiring cleansing.
- (c) Uncertain sequence of phases; nigredo as product of an earlier process, linked to the timelessness of the unconscious.
- (d) Archetype of catastrophic consummation; projection of the unconscious onto others.
- (e)-(g) A man's unconscious appears as anima, a woman's as animus; non-identification as moral effort; the royal game as trans-subjective union, 'opus contra naturam'.
- (h) Conflict and the crucifixion symbol: the natural person must partly die.
- (i)-(j) Conscious realisation of projections as aim; inflation or annihilation of the ego.
- (k)-(m) The king drinks the black water: new birth of the Self; death as conception of the filius philosophorum; union of ego and anima produces the Self.
