# Plate 1: The Mercurial Fountain

## Image
![Plate 1](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS01.jpg)

Image link: [RS01.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS01.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

A square with a star in each corner and a fifth star above its centre. Below the square is a circular basin holding a body of water, which ARAS calls a sea; six stars are placed on the outside of the basin, and an inscription runs along its rim (ARAS refers to it without quoting the text). A fountain rises from the water through three pipes, labelled lac virginis, acetum fontis and aqua vitae. The sun and the moon stand above the three pipes, and a further star sits a little higher still. At the top of the picture a two-headed serpent spits fire from both heads. The water is shown rising and falling back into the basin, forming a loop.

Source of this description: [ARAS concordance](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-1-mercurial-fountain). Inscriptions are as reported by ARAS in English and have not been checked against the print.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the ARAS concordance of Jung's Psychology of the Transference ([ARAS concordance, Rosarium 1](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-1-mercurial-fountain)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- The 1550 edition: Frankfurt, Cyriacus Jacob, printed as part II of De Alchimia Opuscula complura veterum philosophorum, as given on these pages. The other editions and manuscripts below are listed in [Wikipedia, Rosary of the Philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary_of_the_Philosophers); that list has not been checked against the manuscripts themselves.
- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 (English, 18th century, twenty coloured miniatures pasted in, derived with some alteration from the printed version): the three substances of the fountain are labelled Lac Virginis, Acetum fontis and Aqua Vitae, shown mixing in the basin ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)).
- Glasgow MS Ferguson 6 (17th-century German, per Wikipedia's list): the cherub-headed streams are labelled Spiritus, Anima and Corpus ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)).
- Other Glasgow manuscripts on the same exhibition page: MS Ferguson 29 (French, 16th century, twenty black-and-white drawings); MS Ferguson 74 (French, 16th century, twenty-one pen-and-ink drawings); MS Ferguson 96 (Latin, 16th century, Frankfurt illustrations pasted in and coloured); MS Ferguson 149 (German, 16th century, twenty-one illustrations, mostly drawn within flasks). Wikipedia dates Ferguson 29 and 74 to the 18th century and Ferguson 96 and 149 to the 17th; the two sources disagree.
- Printed reprises (Wikipedia): the Artis auriferae (Basel, 1572) reproduced the twenty illustrations as re-cut woodcuts; Johann Daniel Mylius, Philosophia reformata (1622), includes the twenty images redesigned in early 17th-century style by Balthazar Swan.
- Manuscripts (Wikipedia's list): fourteen of the late 16th century, five of the 17th, five of the 18th and four of the 19th. Wikipedia states that none is said to predate the 1550 print.
- Dealer claim: an Inlibris listing for a manuscript by Michael Cochem with a colophon dated 1529 describes the fountain on f. 13 with three liquids, a six-pointed star, sun, moon and a two-headed dragon ([Inlibris listing](https://inlibris.com/item/bn59885/)). This is a bookseller's claim and conflicts with Wikipedia's statement above; it has not been verified.
- Translations (Wikipedia): two excerpts were translated into English in the early 17th century by a pseudonymous woman, "likely Jeanne du Port" in Wikipedia's wording; an 18th-century English translation based on Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 reproduces its coloured drawings as woodcuts; a Czech translation of 1578 by Jaroš Griemiller is held in the National Library in Prague.
