# Plate 8: Purification (Falling Dew)

## Image
Heavenly dew falls on the dead body in the grave. Ros Gedeonis (Gideon's dew) is a synonym for aqua permanens, and so for Mercurius.

## Text and motto
Verse (English rendering quoted by ARAS): "Here falls the heavenly dew, to lave / The soiled black body in the grave". Text quoted by Henderson in connection with this plate: "Whiten the lato and rend the books lest your hearts be rent asunder." Senior (via Maria) describes a heavenly 'king' whose water the earth absorbs, whitening Albira with Astuna.

## Alchemical stage
Albedo (dealbatio), after the nigredo (ARAS)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Purify: Latin purificare, 'render clean', from purus ('pure') and facere ('make') ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/purify)). ARAS titles this plate Purification, and the verse speaks of the dew washing the soiled black body.
- Albedo: a Latinised term meaning 'whiteness', with leucosis as an alternative name; Wikipedia describes the stage as a purification, ablutio, 'the washing away of impurities', and says the dove often stood for it in medieval literature ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo_%28alchemy%29)). ARAS names the stage of this plate albedo (dealbatio).
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶483-492)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (b) The sunrise as the light that follows the darkness; the water as aqua sapientiae.
- (c) Water as symbol of wisdom and spirit: the Samaritan well (John 4:13-14); Cusanus's sermon on three wells (senses, reason, wisdom).
- (d)-(f) The blackness washed away by the aqua sapientiae; extractio animae compared to working through the idea in a dream (Cardan's method); the alchemist aims at a subtle body, analysis at an attitude.
- (g)-(i) A purely intellectual attitude harms emotional life; 'books destroyed' so thinking does not impair feeling; feeling relationships needed beyond merely registering dreams.
- (j) Differentiated, segregated functions lead to neurotic dissociation; the opus also requires love.
- (k) The fourth stage anticipates the lapis, 'the Stone that is no stone'.
