# Plate 9: The Return of the Soul

## Image
The soul descends from on high to revive the dead body that the alchemists had tried to purify. Glasgow MS Ferguson 210, illustration 9 ("Of the rejoicing or springing or sublimation of the soul"): two birds meet above and below ground, showing the soul returning to the cleansed body. ARAS (section a) reads the two birds as the winged and wingless dragons of Mercurius.

## Text and motto
Verse (English rendering quoted by ARAS): "Here is the soul descending from on high / To quick the corpse we strove to purify." Closing verse: "After my many sufferings and great martyry / I rise again transfigured, of all blemish free." Motto from John Gower, Confessio amantis, cited by Jung: "a warring peace, a sweet wound, a mild evil".

## Alchemical stage
Reanimation, following mundificatio (ARAS)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Sublimation: Medieval Latin sublimatio, 'a lifting up', from sublimare ('to raise') and sublimis ('lofty'); in late 14th-century English an alchemical term for purifying a solid by vaporising it and letting it cool ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/sublimation)). Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 captions this illustration 'Of the rejoicing or springing or sublimation of the soul', while the plate itself shows the soul descending to the body.
- Martyr: Late Latin and Doric Greek martys, 'witness'; the sense of someone who dies for a belief grew from bearing witness, and the Greek origin is uncertain ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/martyr)). The closing verse quoted by ARAS speaks of 'my many sufferings and great martyry'.
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶493-524)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) Two birds: winged and wingless dragons, chthonic and pneumatic natures of Mercurius, opposition unresolved.
- (b)-(d) The alchemist's conscious discrimination less acute than modern man's; blurring corpus and spiritus; projection brings the transcendental process into personal life as inflation.
- (e)-(g) Fusion of opposites can dissolve the ego (immunditia); mundificatio as separating the individual from collective truths; the body gives the personality boundaries.
- (h)-(j) Soul reunited with body as 'the one born of the two'; the anima gradually ceasing to act as an autonomous personality; the unintegrated anima as a creature without relationships.
- (k) Animus as woman's discriminating function; female alchemists named: Theosebeia, Paphnutia, Maria Prophetissa, Peronelle, Mrs Atwood.
- (l)-(m) The second series ends with an emperor rather than the first series' empress; the animus in its primary unconscious form.
- (n)-(p) Soul feminine in the man, masculine in the woman; conflict must be held, not resolved; flight from ourselves is futile.
- (q) 'A warring peace, a sweet wound, a mild evil'; cross and risen Christ image.
- (r) Rational analysis of alchemy reaches its limit at opposites.
