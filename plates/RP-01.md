# Plate 1: The Mercurial Fountain

## Image
A square with a star in each corner (the four elements) and a fifth star above the centre (the quinta essentia). Below, a circular basin, the vas Hermeticum, holds the mare nostrum or aqua permanens; six stars on its rim, with Mercurius, make the seven planets/metals. A fountain rises from the basin through three pipes labelled lac virginis, acetum fontis and aqua vitae. Above the pipes stand the sun and moon. At the top a two-headed serpent, the serpens mercurialis, spits fire.

## Text and motto
Opening verse (in the English rendering quoted by ARAS): "We are the metals' first nature and only source". The verse continues with lines on the fountain's healing and poisonous powers; the remainder is not transcribed here (to enter from the 1550 print).

## Alchemical stage
Prima materia; Mercurius as source (no single operation named by ARAS)

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶402-409)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) The basin: the vas Hermeticum as matrix uniting the hostile elements of the square; Mercurius as pater metallorum, a unity of the seven planets.
- (b) Three manifestations of Mercurius: the fountain as a triad, the chthonic counterpart of the heavenly Trinity.
- (c) The divided serpent: serpens bifidus as the duplex natura of Mercurius; linked to the 'duo fumi' of Maria the Copt.
- (d) Axiom of Maria: the process as 4 > 3 > 2 > 1, from chaos to the filius regius.
- (e) Odd and even numbers: odd as masculine, even as feminine.
- (f) Quaternity as an ordering schema; the circle as completeness.
- (g) Triad as agens, dyad as patiens.
- (h) The picture as the unconscious psyche projected into matter; (h-1) Goethe's Faust; (h-2) Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosencreutz (1616) as forerunner of Faust with the same Axioma Mariae.
- (i) 'Water' of the Art signifies the unconscious (synonyms: medicina catholica, aqua Saturni, vinum ardens).
- (j) Gushing up and flowing back completes a circle; linked to Nicholas of Cusa's image of God.

## Names and works cited
Dorn, Maria the Copt, Nicholas of Cusa, Dante, Goethe (Faust), Chymical Wedding (1616); Jung refers onward to 'The Spirit Mercurius'.

## Other witnesses and notes
- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 (18th-c. English copy): the three substances are labelled Lac Virginis, Acetum fontis, Aqua Vitae, mixing in the basin to form the water of Mercury. MS Ferguson 6 (17th-c. German) labels the cherub-headed streams Spiritus, Anima, Corpus.
- The earliest dated manuscript witness claimed for the Rosarium (Michael Cochem, colophon 1529, Inlibris listing) has the fountain on f. 13, described with three liquids, a six-pointed star, sun, moon and a two-headed dragon. Dealer claim; Wikipedia states no manuscript predates the 1550 print.
- Modern dissenting reading (blog essay, Benoit Rousseau, 7 March 2025, gnosisjung.org): argues Jung's reading 'does not make sense' (no explanation of why chaos is linked to four; the three faucets and the 'inorganic world' in humans left unidentified) and proposes that the four elements correspond to four cognitive functions, the sun to the dominant function, the moon to the three undeveloped ones, and the quintessence to all four operating; compares Greek four-part schemes (temperaments, humours) and Myers-Briggs typing. This is one author's interpretation, not established scholarship. The essay also lists Mercurius's alternate names: mare nostrum, aqua permanens, mare tenebrosum. It quotes Jung (CW16 ¶404) on the 'tetrameria' of the transforming process and the 'pluralistic state of the man who has not yet attained inner unity'.
