# Plate 10: The New Birth (Apotheosis of the Rebis)

## Image
![Plate 10](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS10.jpg)

Image link: [RS10.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS10.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

A winged figure, the right side of its body male and the left side female, stands on a moon. In one hand it holds a chalice containing three snakes (or possibly one three-headed snake); in the other it holds a single snake. To the left of the figure is a raven; to the right is a tree bearing sun and moon. ARAS gives the caption verse in English and does not state further details of the setting.

Source of this description: [ARAS concordance](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-10-new-birth). Inscriptions are as reported by ARAS in English and have not been checked against the print.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the ARAS concordance of Jung's Psychology of the Transference ([ARAS concordance, Rosarium 10](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-10-new-birth)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210, illustration 10: the figure stands on a crescent moon with a moon tree and a bird, and is winged; a detail is labelled "the Moon Tree" ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)).
- Wikipedia states that woodcuts 10, 17 and 19 have direct precedents in the Buch der heiligen Dreifaltigkeit (Book of the Holy Trinity), an early 15th-century German alchemical treatise attributed to the Franciscan Frater Ulmannus. Wikipedia gives its earliest manuscript (Berlin) as 1410-1419, with a revision of 1433 for Johannes von Bayreuth, and says some of its drawings reappear in later 16th-century works including the 1550 Rosarium ([Wikipedia, Rosary of the Philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary_of_the_Philosophers)). Whether the precedent images match closely has not been checked.
