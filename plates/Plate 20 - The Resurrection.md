# Plate 20: The Resurrection

## Image
The resurrected Christ emerges from the sarcophagus. In Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 he is given a radiant sun head.

## Text and motto
No verse or motto cited in the sources consulted.

## Alchemical stage
Not named; the filius philosophorum (Henderson)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Resurrection: Church Latin resurrectionem, 'a rising again from the dead', from resurgere, 're-' ('again') and surgere ('to rise') ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/resurrection)). On this plate the resurrected Christ emerges from the sarcophagus (Glasgow: with a radiant sun head).
- Philosopher: Greek philosophos, 'lover of wisdom', from philos ('loving') and sophos ('wise'); in the Middle Ages the word could also mean an alchemist or magician ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/philosopher)). Henderson names the figure the 'filius philosophorum', the son of the philosopher.
- Sarcophagus: Greek sarkophagos, 'flesh-eating' limestone used for coffins ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/sarcophagus)). On this plate the resurrected Christ emerges from the sarcophagus (ARAS summary of Henderson).
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.
