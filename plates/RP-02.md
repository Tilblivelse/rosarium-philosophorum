# Plate 2: King and Queen

## Image
Rex and Regina, bridegroom and bride, approach each other for betrothal. Each stands on a celestial body: the king on the sun, the queen on the moon. They wear court dress and touch left hands, then right hands. The right hands hold a device of five flowers: four branches bearing two flowers each, and a fifth flower brought from above by a dove descending from a quintessential star (see Plate 1).

## Text and motto
ARAS quotes Senior: "Aurum nostrum non est aurum vulgi" (our gold is not common gold). Phrases in the commentary: lumen de lumine; umbra solis / sol niger; donum Spiritus Sancti; opus magnum. A full verse text is not reproduced on the ARAS page. Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 gives scrolls on the following plate (see Plate 3).

## Alchemical stage
Coniunctio announced (ARAS names no operation; frames the figure around the coniunctio)

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶410-449)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) The pair meets for betrothal; brother-sister relationship (Apollo and Diana) implies incest. (a-1) Left-hand contact: secret, affective bond; Jung links it to Tantric 'left-hand path'. (a-2) Right hands: compensatory; four flowers = four elements, fifth from the dove = quintessence.
- (b) Union of right hands mediated by the Holy Spirit; recapitulates the opus of Plate 1.
- (c)-(d) Nature versus spirit; the royal relationship as natural, tied to unconscious contents.
- (e) The coniunctio not legitimate, essentially incestuous; tied to Freud's 'fear of incest'.
- (f)-(g) Mercurius triple-natured; the imprisoned Primordial Man (Anthropos); Zosimos, Waite, Wei Po-yang (chen-jen).
- (h) Danger of heresy for the alchemists; Rosarium and Aurora consurgens do not settle how conscious they were.
- (i) Incest as union with one's own being; (i-1) 'Visio Arislei' like-with-like union.
- (j) Transference parallel; (j-1) familiarisation; (j-2) shadow as prima materia (umbra solis / sol niger); (j-3) facing the shadow.
- (k) Counter-crossing of the sexes: the king represents the female soror, the queen the male adept (anima/animus projections); (k-1) royal figures as collective archetypes.
- (l) Four relationship types: personal; man-anima / woman-animus; anima-animus; identity with the contrasexual figure; (l-3) marriage quaternio in folklore.
- (m)-(p) Endogamy and exogamy, cross-cousin marriage, two crossing brother-sister marriages as a model for the alchemical quaternio; group marriage, Spencer and Gillen, Layard; incest prerogative of kings passing to the gods (hierosgamos).
- (q)-(x) Endogamous tendency finding spiritual outlet; unintegrated anima/animus in Christian civilisation; only the incest taboo remains; need for conscious inner consolidation; kinship libido persisting after projection fades; transference regression and collapse.
- (y)-(z) Two aspects of individuation (inner integration, objective relationship), each with its danger; the transference bond as a societal value, an opus magnum.
