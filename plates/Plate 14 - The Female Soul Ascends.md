# Plate 14: The Female Soul Ascends

## Image
The hermaphrodite lies in the sarcophagus; a female soul figure ascends from it.

## Text and motto
No verse or motto cited in the sources consulted.

## Alchemical stage
Nigredo, the depressive phase (Henderson)

### Etymology and symbolism
- Anima: Latin, the feminine form of animus, from a root meaning 'to breathe'; Jung adopted it in 1923 for the feminine component of a man's psyche ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/anima)). Henderson reads the ascending female soul of this plate as 'very much the man's anima figure - the soul as feminine'.
- Nigredo: Henderson calls this the depressive phase of the nigredo; Wikipedia glosses the term as 'blackness' and records its psychological sense as the 'dark night of the soul' ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigredo)).
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Reading (Henderson, second series)
Joseph L. Henderson, presentation of 18 March 1990 on the second half of the series, summarised by Daniel S. Benveniste (ARAS Connections 2023, Issue 2).

- Henderson: the soul here is 'very much the man's anima figure - the soul as feminine'. In deep depression the patient may feel the will to live is lost; the right attitude is patience and attentiveness, not avoidance or enactment, until a new meaning appears at 'the dawn of a new day'.
