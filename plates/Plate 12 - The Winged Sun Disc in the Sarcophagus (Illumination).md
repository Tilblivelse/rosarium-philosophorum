# Plate 12: The Winged Sun Disc in the Sarcophagus (Illumination)

## Image
A winged sun disc in a sarcophagus.

## Text and motto
No verse or motto cited in the sources consulted.

## Alchemical stage
Beginning of a death cycle; 'goal of individuation' (Henderson); Glasgow caption 'Illumination'

## Reading (Henderson, second series)
Joseph L. Henderson, presentation of 18 March 1990 on the second half of the series, summarised by Daniel S. Benveniste (ARAS Connections 2023, Issue 2).

- Henderson: individuation is not endlessly repetitive nor an ideal reached 'up in heaven somewhere'; the wings are the wings of spirit and the disc a symbol of the Self. He deferred this plate to the end of his presentation.

## Jung
Jung's Psychology of the Transference (1946) comments on plates 1-10 only.

## Other witnesses and notes
- Glasgow: caption 'Illumination'; the solar element leaves the body and ascends; the body is cleansed with rain or dew before the soul returns; the hermaphrodite loses its female side and wings, representing sacrifice.
- Adam McLean, commentary in the Magnum Opus edition of the Rosary of the Philosophers (Edinburgh, 1980), hosted on alchemywebsite.com. Layer 3, one author's view. McLean reads all twenty woodcuts as one pattern (Body, Soul and Spirit) and presents his reading as one interpretation among others. What he says about this plate specifically has not yet been read. Full entry: [[Plate 1 - The Mercurial Fountain|Plate 1: The Mercurial Fountain]].
- Henderson (ARAS summary), fuller: individuation is something alive that comes and goes, neither a repetition nor a fixed ideal in heaven; the winged sun disc is a symbol of the Self, a unified duality of the worldly creature and the sun, beyond understanding yet necessary for life.
- Glasgow overlap: the exhibition page describes the soul's return under illustration 12 and again under illustration 9 ([[Plate 9 - The Return of the Soul|Plate 9: The Return of the Soul]]) and does not clearly separate them.
