# Plate 17: The Demonstration of Perfection (Bat-winged Hermaphrodite)

## Image
![Plate 17](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS17.jpg)

Image link: [RS17.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS17.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

A bat-winged hermaphrodite in royal vestments stands on a three-headed monster that eats itself. It holds a snake in its left hand and a chalice with a three-headed snake in its right. In the background are a hillside, a bird in a nest pecking at its breast with two young catching drops of blood, a lion, and a tree with six pairs of sun-heads and a single sun-head on top.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the summary of Joseph L. Henderson's 1990 presentation by Daniel S. Benveniste (ARAS Connections 2023, issue 2) ([ARAS, On the Rosarium Philosophorum (Benveniste/Henderson)](https://aras.org/documents/rosarium-philosophorum)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 caption: "The Demonstration of Perfection - a riot of alchemical imagery with Lion, Sun Tree, Pelican and Three-headed Serpent" ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)). The page attaches the Sun Tree detail and the same title to illustration 15 as well; see the Plate 15 page.
- Wikipedia states that woodcuts 10 and 17 depict the "Hermetic androgyne" and have direct precedents in the Buch der heiligen Dreifaltigkeit, an early 15th-century German treatise (earliest manuscript, Berlin, 1410-1419, per Wikipedia) ([Wikipedia, Rosary of the Philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary_of_the_Philosophers)). Whether the precedent images match closely has not been checked.
