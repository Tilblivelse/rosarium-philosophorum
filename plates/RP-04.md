# Plate 4: Immersion in the Bath

## Image
The king and queen descend into a bath or watery 'sea'; a dove above; a starry symbol held by both. (ARAS gives no direct description of the image; this is reconstructed from its commentary. Direct description to enter from the print.)

## Text and motto
The text accompanying the picture, as quoted by ARAS: "Our Stone is to be extracted from the nature of the two bodies." No separate motto.

## Alchemical stage
Solutio (dissolution), identified in ARAS section (c)

## Jungian reading (Psychology of the Transference, CW16 ¶453-456)
Section summaries follow the ARAS concordance; lettering is ARAS's.

- (a) The rising unconscious fountain has reached the royal pair, who descend into it like a bath.
- (b) Watery Mercurius attacks from below as the dove came from above.
- (c) Solutio: dissolution, return to a dark initial state like amniotic fluid; the Stone grows like a child in the womb.
- (d) Dorn, Speculativae philosophiae: dissolving bodies likened to resolving the philosophers' doubts through knowledge.
- (e) The Stone drawn from two natures; water linked to the ventus of the Tabula smaragdina, to soul as 'oil and water'; mingling echoed in the chalice of the Mass.
- (f) Alchemy and the Mass: the coniunctio in the Grandes heures du duc de Berry; Melchior Cibinensis; the soul as hermaphroditic link between body (Venus) and spirit (Mercurius).
- (g) Soul as bond between body and spirit; wholeness requires a relationship with a 'You'.
- (h) Night sea journey and descent into the unconscious; Visio Arislei; Heraclitus on water and soul; fiery Mercurius likened to libido.
- (i) The scene recapitulates the Original Man (Nous) descending and embracing Physis; modern equivalent in sexual fantasy colouring the transference.

## Names and works cited
Mercurius, Dorn, Heraclitus, Gabricus (Thabritius), Beya, Nous, Physis, Melchior Cibinensis, Rex Marinus, Visio Arislei, Tabula smaragdina, Grandes heures du duc de Berry, the Uroboros.
