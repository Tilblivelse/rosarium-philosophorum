# Plate 2: King and Queen

## Image
![Plate 2](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS02.jpg)

Image link: [RS02.jpg](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/virtual_museum/Images/RS02.jpg) (hosted by alchemywebsite.com; no licence stated)

Two figures in court dress, a king and a queen (bridegroom and bride), stand apart, each on a celestial body: the king on the sun, the queen on the moon. They clasp left hands, and their right hands together hold a single device of five flowers. Four of the flowers grow on branches, two to a branch; the fifth is carried from above by a dove that descends from a star. ARAS also mentions three branches, but its text does not say clearly how they relate to this picture.

Source of this description: [ARAS concordance](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-2-king-and-queen). Inscriptions are as reported by ARAS in English and have not been checked against the print.

## Provenance of this description
The description above is paraphrased from the ARAS concordance of Jung's Psychology of the Transference ([ARAS concordance, Rosarium 2](https://aras.org/concordance/rosarium-2-king-and-queen)). It has not been checked against the 1550 print. The title is the conventional title used in that source, not a caption from the print.

The English text of the whole work is on [alchemywebsite.com](https://alchemywebsite.com/rosary0.html), described there as a transcription of the English translation in MS Ferguson 210. It is a later English translation, not the 1550 Latin and German. The page names no translator or editor and states no rights; booksellers list Adam McLean's Magnum Opus Hermetic Sourceworks No. 6 as an edited English translation from that manuscript.

## Documentary notes
Each item is attributed to its source and describes a witness or a fact about the edition, not a reading of the picture. Glasgow's captions describe the 18th-century MS Ferguson 210 copy, not the 1550 print.

- Glasgow MS Ferguson 210, illustration 2: captioned as the king and queen personifying the solar and lunar forces; illustrations 2 and 3 show the two offering flowers to each other, with a dove bringing a third flower ([Glasgow, MS Ferguson 210](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/month/april2009.html)). The page reports the scroll texts in a passage covering illustrations 2 and 3 together, so which picture carries which scroll is unclear.
