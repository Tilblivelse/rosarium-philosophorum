# Plate 11: Sol and Luna in the Waters (Fermentation)

## Image
Sol and Luna copulate in the waters. Luna is on top; both are winged.

## Text and motto
No verse or motto cited in the sources consulted.

## Alchemical stage
Coniunctio (Henderson links the image to the coniunctio); Glasgow caption 'Fermentation'

### Etymology and symbolism
- Ferment: Latin fermentare, 'to leaven, cause to rise', from fermentum, 'leaven' or a fermented drink, possibly shortened from fervimentum and linked to fervere, 'to boil, seethe'; 'fermentation' entered English in the late 14th century as an alchemical term ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/ferment)). Glasgow MS Ferguson 210 captions this plate 'Fermentation' and treats it as the opening of the second cycle.
- Etymologies are from the sources linked here. Where a plate source (ARAS, Glasgow, Henderson) uses the term, the link to the plate is that source's; whether any source draws a symbolic connection from the word's origin itself has not been checked.

## Reading (Henderson, second series)
Joseph L. Henderson, presentation of 18 March 1990 on the second half of the series, summarised by Daniel S. Benveniste (ARAS Connections 2023, Issue 2).

- Henderson: parallels the frequent emergence of an incestuous undercurrent or erotic transference in analysis ('The incestuous undercurrent is an important theme throughout Jung's treatment of the transference').

## Jung
Jung's Psychology of the Transference (1946) comments on plates 1-10 only.

## Other witnesses and notes
- Glasgow: caption 'Fermentation'; opens the second cycle (plates 11-17), dealing with mastery of the solar element or Red Stone; images 5 and 11 both show the conjunction. A version of this image was used as cover art for Leonard Cohen's 'New Skin for Old Ceremony' (a wing moved to cover the figures' abdominal areas; later withdrawn).
- Adam McLean, commentary in the Magnum Opus edition of the Rosary of the Philosophers (Edinburgh, 1980), hosted on alchemywebsite.com. Layer 3, one author's view. McLean reads all twenty woodcuts as one pattern (Body, Soul and Spirit) and presents his reading as one interpretation among others. What he says about this plate specifically has not yet been read. Full entry: [[Plate 1 - The Mercurial Fountain|Plate 1: The Mercurial Fountain]].
- Glasgow: the female figure is active in this conjunction, whereas in illustration 5 she is passive.
- Henderson (ARAS summary), fuller: the therapist faces a dilemma, since cutting off the erotic transference and acting on it both stop the process; the therapist must stay with the patient until it transforms. This extends the line given under the Henderson reading above.
- Karen-Claire Voss (1990) reads the series through the hierosgamos theme; see [[Plate 1 - The Mercurial Fountain|Plate 1: The Mercurial Fountain]]. Not consulted.
